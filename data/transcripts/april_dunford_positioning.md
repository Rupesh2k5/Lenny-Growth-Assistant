## Transcript Excerpts

### On the 5 Components of Positioning
**April Dunford**:
"Most founders confuse positioning with messaging, taglines, or branding. Positioning is the fundamental context that makes your product's unique value obvious to your best customers.

There are five interlocking components you must define in strict sequential order:

1. **Competitive Alternatives**: What would your target customers use if your product did not exist? (Often it's not a direct competitor—it's Excel, manual email threads, hiring an intern, or doing nothing).
2. **Unique Attributes / Capabilities**: What technical features, architectural advantages, or capabilities do you have that the competitive alternatives do not?
3. **Value Delivered**: What business or emotional outcome do those unique capabilities unlock for the customer? (Features tell, but value sells).
4. **Ideal Customer Profile (ICP)**: Which specific segment of customers cares deeply about that specific value and is willing to pay premium prices for it right now?
5. **Market Category**: What frame of reference (e.g. 'Data Warehouse', 'CRM for Real Estate', 'Async Video Messenger') helps buyers instantly understand what you are and sets the right expectations for features and pricing?"

### On Why Creating a New Category is Often a Trap
**April Dunford**:
"Venture capitalists love telling founders: 'Go create a whole new category!' But category creation is the most expensive, high-risk go-to-market strategy in tech. You have to educate the market on why the problem exists, convince them of your new terminology, and defend the space when giants copy you.

In 90% of cases, you are much better off positioning your product into an existing, well-understood market category with a sharp sub-segment differentiator (e.g. 'A CRM built specifically for High-Volume B2B Logistics') than trying to invent a new three-letter acronym nobody searches for on Google."
