## Transcript Excerpts

### On Designing the 11-Star Experience
**Brian Chesky**: 
"If you want to build something that is truly viral and creates word-of-mouth growth, you have to design what I call the 11-star experience. 

A 5-star experience is: you knock on the door, they open the door, they let you in, the room is clean, the sheets are fresh. It's good, you leave a 5-star review, but you don't tell all your friends.

A 6-star experience: they let you in, there's a bottle of wine on the table, a welcome card, fresh fruit. You're delighted.

A 7-star experience: the host knows you like surfing, so they leave a custom surfboard, a wetsuit in your size, and a list of the best secret surf spots.

An 8-star experience: you arrive at the airport, a limousine is waiting for you with your favorite snacks, and takes you directly to the home.

A 9-star experience: you land, 5,000 screaming fans are waiting at the gate holding signs with your name.

An 10-star experience: you arrive, and Elon Musk is waiting in a spaceship to take you to Mars.

An 11-star experience: you arrive, and the host is a hologram of your childhood hero, welcoming you to an interdimensional adventure.

Now, obviously 9, 10, and 11 are ridiculous. But the point of this exercise is that 5 stars is standard, 6 is nice, 7 or 8 is sweet spot where you find the magical moment. You deliberately stretch your imagination to the extreme, find the edge of what's possible, and then pull back to the sweet spot (6-8 stars) that is feasible, economical, and extraordinarily memorable."

### On "Founder Mode" and Product Management
**Brian Chesky**:
"Traditional management advice says: hire great executives, give them autonomy, get out of their way, and let them run their divisions. We tried that around 2018-2019, and Airbnb became a matrixed, slow, bureaucratic company where product managers were acting like mini-CEOs arguing over headcount and turf instead of shipping great design.

When the pandemic hit, we eliminated the traditional division-led PM structure. We got rid of the classic Product Manager title as it existed and combined Product Management with Product Marketing. We put designers at the center of the roadmap.

Every six months, we do two major seasonal releases—Summer Release and Winter Release. I personally review every single screen and feature that goes into the release. That's what people call 'Founder Mode'. It's not micromanagement; it's deep engagement with the details of your craft. If the leadership doesn't care about the pixels, the copy, and the latency, nobody down the line will either."

### On Deciding What NOT to Build
**Brian Chesky**:
"Most startups die of indigestion, not starvation. They try to build 10 features at once because three enterprise customers asked for them. 

At Airbnb, we had to kill dozens of sub-projects (like Airbnb Flights, luxury hotels, standalone experiences) to refocus 100% of our energy on the core host and guest experience. If your core product isn't 10x better than the alternative, no adjacent feature will save you. Focus is saying no to 20 great ideas so you can execute the one transformative idea flawlessly."
