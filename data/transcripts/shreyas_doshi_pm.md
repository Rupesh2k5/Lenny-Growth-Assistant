## Transcript Excerpts

### On the LNO Framework for PM Time & Quality Management
**Shreyas Doshi**:
"Most PMs burn out because they treat every task with identical 100% intensity. They spend four hours formatting a status update slide deck, and then have zero cognitive energy left to do deep strategic thinking on product positioning.

I created the **LNO Framework** to categorize all work into three distinct buckets:

1. **L Tasks (Leverage Work)**:
   - High-impact, disproportionate leverage activities (e.g. core PRDs, strategy memos, critical UX wireframes, pricing decisions).
   - Expected standard: Perfection / 10x excellence. You should spend 60% of your creative energy here.
2. **N Tasks (Neutral Work)**:
   - Standard operational tasks required to keep the lights on (e.g. sprint planning, bug triage, vendor evaluations, routine metric updates).
   - Expected standard: 'Good enough' / 80% quality. Do it cleanly, quickly, and move on.
3. **O Tasks (Overhead Work)**:
   - Low-value bureaucratic chores (e.g. filling out expense reports, broad informational status emails, attending non-essential multi-department syncs).
   - Expected standard: Active minimization / deliberate imperfection / batching / delegating.

If you don't intentionally do 'O' tasks with 50% effort, you will never have the space to do 'L' tasks with 10x excellence."

### On High Agency vs Low Agency in Product Management
**Shreyas Doshi**:
"High agency is the ability to bend reality to your will. When a low-agency PM encounters a roadblock—say, legal says 'No, you cannot launch this signup flow'—they throw their hands up and report to leadership: 'We cannot launch because Legal blocked us.'

A high-agency PM asks: 'What specific risk is Legal worried about? Can we solve their regulatory concern by adding a 1-sentence disclosure tooltip? Can I set up a 15-minute working session with the compliance officer to co-design an alternative?' 

High-agency leaders find a path through ambiguity and constraints without waiting for permission or escalating every blocker to their VP."

### On Running Pre-Mortems to Prevent Failure
**Shreyas Doshi**:
"Post-mortems are done after a project dies. Pre-mortems are done before the project launches while the patient is still alive and healthy.

Before building a major feature or launching a new product:
1. Gather the core team (Engineering, Design, Data, Sales, Legal).
2. Tell everyone: 'Imagine it is 6 months from now. We launched this product, and it was a complete and catastrophic disaster. Users hated it, churn spiked, and leadership cancelled the roadmap. Take 10 minutes silently writing down the exact reasons why it failed.'
3. Collect all responses on a whiteboard or Miro board. Group them into themes.
4. Convert those failure modes into proactive mitigation tasks in the pre-launch sprint plan."
