### On the 40% Product-Market Fit Survey
**Sean Ellis**:
"People talk about Product-Market Fit as a vague mystical feeling. I wanted an objective, quantifiable leading indicator before spending money on user acquisition.

I developed the PMF Survey question:
*'How would you feel if you could no longer use [Product] tomorrow?'*
- A) Very disappointed
- B) Somewhat disappointed
- C) Not disappointed (it isn't really that useful)
- D) N/A - I no longer use it

I benchmarked this across hundreds of startups including Dropbox, Eventbrite, and LogMeIn. If **40% or more** of your active users answer **'Very Disappointed'**, you have achieved product-market fit and can aggressively scale acquisition.

If your score is below 40% (say 20%), pouring money into growth is like pouring water into a leaky bucket. You will churn users as fast as you acquire them."

### On the ICE Scoring Model for Growth Experimentation
**Sean Ellis**:
"When running a high-velocity growth team, you have dozens of experiment ideas each week. You need a fast, objective prioritization system. We use **ICE Scoring**:

1. **Impact (1-10)**: If this experiment succeeds, how dramatically will it improve our North Star Metric?
2. **Confidence (1-10)**: How confident are we that this hypothesis is correct, based on user research, data, or prior benchmark tests?
3. **Ease (1-10)**: How simple and fast is this to implement? (A 10 is a copy change in Webflow; a 1 is a 3-month backend database refactor).

Score = $(Impact + Confidence + Ease) / 3$. 

Rank your backlog by ICE score every Monday morning. Commit to shipping the top 3 highest-scoring tests during the weekly Growth Sprint."

### On Defining a True North Star Metric
**Sean Ellis**:
"A North Star Metric is not simply 'Monthly Revenue' or 'Gross Merchandise Value'. Revenue is an output metric for the company, not a reflection of value delivered to the customer.

A true North Star Metric captures the moment when customer value and business value intersect:
- **Airbnb**: Nights booked (captures host revenue and guest travel delight).
- **Spotify**: Time spent listening (captures listener engagement and artist royalties).
- **Dropbox**: Daily active users who saved a file across multiple synced devices.

When everyone from engineering to marketing rallies around one metric that reflects customer value, cross-functional prioritization becomes clear."
